# Attribute Concatenation

*On crossing the bridge.*

Anatomy is a structure. Status is where that structure starts to live.

- **ATTRIBUTES STATUS**: EIC step that studies the <plain>interstitial physiology processes of Behavior Dynamics</plain>.

![Diagram showing the components of Attributes Status](../../diagrams/svg/attribute-status.svg)

As in Attributes Structure, we apply operators to the primary pairs. This time the operator is not accentuation *inside* a pair. It is concatenation *between* attributes of different pairs.

- **ATTRIBUTE CONCATENATION**: Crossed <plain>interaction between attribute pairs from the primary jurisdiction</plain>.

| **ATTRIBUTE CONCATENATION** | **SYMBOLIC REPRESENTATION** |
| --- | --- |
| Stillness ^ Simultaneity = Consistency | Q <plain>^</plain> Si = C |
| Movement ^ Successiveness = Impermanence | M <plain>^</plain> Su = I |
| Stillness ^ Successiveness = Balance | Q <plain>^</plain> Su = B |
| Movement ^ Simultaneity = Imbalance | M <plain>^</plain> Si = b |

The result is not four equal capacities, each an "enhanced" version of a primary (<plain>Stillness → Pro-Stillness</plain>). It is two new pairs, of two different kinds:

- Consistency / Impermanence
- Balance / Imbalance

The first pair **reinforces**. Successive motion leads to **Impermanence**; simultaneous serenity leads to **Consistency**. A held tone, a law in force at noon and at midnight, a stone that is still *now*: <plain>consistency</plain>. A fever's course, a price series, a river: <plain>impermanence</plain>. These qualities attach themselves spontaneously, but the pairing is far less stable than anything in the primary jurisdiction. Accentuating one side here is more violent. It has a greater risk of breaking the pair.

The second pair **cancels**. That is why its members are **interstitial statuses** rather than identifiable qualities. A state of **Balance** depends on remaining still through the passage of time — a fallow field, a gyroscope, a clause that is not rewritten every week. **Imbalance** is simultaneous elements moving differently: two agencies on the same file, an ensemble out of time, many hands on one bed each working a different motion. <plain>Balance</plain> does not mean superiority. There are contexts that need <plain>imbalance</plain>.

With these two pairs in place, their further <plain>interactions</plain> will produce a higher — and less stable — interstitial complexity.
