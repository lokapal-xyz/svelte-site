# Agent-Based Models

*On targets and attractors.*

Most classic agent-based models are engineered to reach a stable attractor. The simulation continues until change falls to zero, and the halted state is treated as the outcome of interest. When that state is also regarded as desirable or functional, the combination reads as perdurable <plain>functionality</plain>.

This conflicts with the <plain>Incompatibility Axiom. Functionality and Perdurability are not compatible in a mixed physiology system. Functionality longing predisposes Imperdurability; Perdurability longing predisposes Dysfunctionality. The conciliation pathway is defined as Iterated Imperdurable Functionality, not as a stable end-state that both works and lasts.</plain>

Using equilibrium-seeking models to showcase the framework therefore creates an incongruence of authority. The model's own success condition quietly reintroduces the compatibility that the treatise intentionally denies. Native models should avoid the incongruence by taking the synthesis distinctions as the organizing principle from the outset rather than as vocabulary added to an existing family.

| Synthesis | Defining Longing | Characteristic Tendency | Computational Implication |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Instrumental** | <plain>Functionality</plain> | Optimal performance and successful outcomes | Success condition is functional completion |
| **Organic** | <plain>Perdurability</plain> | Personal preservation and communal endurance | Success condition is continued existence |
| **Mixed** | Both | Struggle between <plain>Functionality and Perdurability</plain> | Success condition requires sustainable iteration |

The three synthesis tracks remain available. Instrumental models treat functional completion as success. Organic models treat continued existence as success. Mixed models require that functional arrangements be succeeded rather than preserved. Most existing agent-based practice falls readily into the Instrumental line. Attempts to add noise, turnover, or endogenous change are often incomplete moves that place organic pressure on an instrumental base. The Organic line is comparatively empty, mostly represented by models in the biological and ecological sciences.

Mixed Synthesis is the most demanding and, for many purposes, the most interesting. It is not superior to the other two. If not constructed properly, the result may feel like an Instrumental model with a Mixed interpretation placed on top. Systems that are effectively instrumental or effectively organic exist and are worth modeling on their own terms. An all-encompassing framework leaves room for all three. Therefore, the synthesis track should be carefully selected.

## Modeling Target and Modeling Mechanics

Every model requires an explicit **modeling target**: a concrete phenomenon, process, or <plain>operation</plain> that the agents act upon and that can be observed independently of the synthesis framework itself.

The synthesis type (Instrumental, Organic, or Mixed) supplies the longing and the success condition. It is the framework, not the subject. Without a distinct target, the model can only restate its own longing; there is no external <plain>criterion</plain> against which to judge whether the implementation is doing anything more than narrating the framework. The result remains empty.

The target must be specific enough that the longing produces visible, inspectable differences in dynamics. It must not be a <plain>Conciliatorics</plain> concept (the longing, the axiom, the pathway, <plain>Operational Status</plain>, etc.). Choosing and stating the target is a prerequisite for writing any starting spec. Later increments modify how agents pursue or are constrained by that same target; they do not replace the need for it.

**Modeling mechanics** are the concrete rules by which agents enact the <plain>modeling target</plain>. They include whatever update rules, thresholds, resources, penalties, or constraint sets the chosen target requires. Mechanics may vary by track and level; the target itself remains fixed for a given family of models.

A model is assigned to one or more synthesis tracks only when the chosen target and mechanics can be realized under those longings without redefinition. The assignment is an informed match between target and track, not a requirement that every target occupy every track.
