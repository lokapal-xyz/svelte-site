# Computational Systemics

*On native arrangements.*

Edified Intrinsic Transformation engages the inherent properties of concrete technical things and performs something with them. <plain>Computational Systemics</plain> is one such engagement. It is the use of systems-specific principles to organize computational arrangements so that those arrangements remain congruent with the framework in use rather than merely decorated by it.

- **COMPUTATIONAL SYSTEMICS**: The computational development of models, simulations, and multi-agent arrangements that engage the perspectives grouped under <plain>Systemics</plain>.

It is the computational counterpart of the meta-discipline itself. Just as Systemics gathers General Systems Theory, Systems Science, cybernetics, ecology, and other approaches without forcing them into a single vocabulary, Computational Systemics gathers the corresponding computational work—computational General Systems Theory, computational Systems Science, and so on—without requiring that work to pass through every perspective. <plain>Computational Conciliatorics</plain> is the subset of that landscape organized by <plain>Conciliatorics</plain> distinctions.

- **COMPUTATIONAL CONCILIATORICS**: The development of computational models and multi-agent systems according to <plain>Conciliatorics</plain> principles, such as synthesis distinctions, longings, and operational gradations.

When the <plain>Conciliatorics</plain> context is clear, <plain>*Computational Systemics* and *Computational Conciliatorics*</plain> are interchangeable. But because there could be other <plain>Systemics</plain> frameworks that implement computational models and systems, the conceptual distinction is necessary to maintain jurisdictional discipline.

## Computational Landscapes

We'll present two landscapes ready to be developed: Agent-Based Models (ABMs) and Multi-Agent Systems (MASs). They share a computational <plain>medium</plain> and a platform for concrete implementation, yet their purposes differ.

In the first landscape the computational <plain>arrangement</plain> is a means. Agents act on a chosen <plain>phenomenon—the modeling target—so</plain> that the differences produced by Instrumental, Organic, or Mixed longings become visible and inspectable. The value lies in what the dynamics show about that target. Once the run ends, the <plain>arrangement</plain> itself has done its job.

In the second landscape the computational <plain>arrangement</plain> is itself the subject. The agents, their differentiated roles, the constraints under which they continue or cease to act, and the way work passes among them are the things being shaped. An external task is still required, yet the primary interest is whether the multi-agent anatomy and physiology can be enhanced. The <plain>arrangement</plain> does not terminate when a single task ends; it remains available for the next one.

Both landscapes are representational. Neither proves the Incompatibility Axiom, the conciliation pathway, or any other claim advanced in the treatise. A computational <plain>system</plain> that runs under <plain>Conciliatorics-native</plain> rules can show that the rules are implementable and that certain dynamics appear when they are followed. It cannot confer additional authority on the rules. The argument stays with the treatise. The systems stay downstream of it.

This boundary is stated because computational arrangements are often treated as stronger evidence than they are, especially in domains that lack laboratory predictability. When a model or a multi-agent run produces a recognizable pattern, it is easy to treat the output as confirmation of the idea that motivated it. Computational Conciliatorics intentionally declines that step. The computational arrangements illustrate and explore. They do not establish.
