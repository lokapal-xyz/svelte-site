# Multi-Agent Systems

*On roles, budgets, and continued participation.*

Multi-agent systems (MASs) already possess a practical surface. Agents are given roles, handed tasks, and expected to return results under real constraints of context, rate, and continued availability. A MAS whose agents must acquire and expend a shared, scarce computational resource (for example, a limited token budget) is already expressing a Mixed Synthesis. The resource is the medium through which both longings are realized. An agent that cannot access token resources cannot continue to participate. An agent that expends them poorly may remain active yet fail to produce effective results. 

The tension between Functionality and Perdurability is not narrated in a MAS; it is the condition under which action occurs. Therefore, Computational Systemics can focus on specific Conciliatorics principles besides Attribute Synthesis categorizations. The following is a brief rundown of the main bridges between Conciliatorics and MASs.

## Relation to Behavior Dynamics

In this arrangement the computational setup can be read directly through Behavior Dynamics:

- **Members** are the agents. Their continued capacity to act depends on access to the shared resource.
- **Medium** includes the shared computational budget, modular components, network infrastructure, and so on.
- **Behavior** is the transformation of that Medium by the agents according to their instructions and goals.
- **System** is the full infrastructure that encloses agents and medium, and allows them to connect to other systems.

These elements can be applied to different multi-agent architectures. A specialized MAS that focuses on one specific behavior can be also a sub-agent for a larger MAS—both system and member respectively and concurrently.

## Relation to Operational Status

The Operation compass supplies a native role structure. This systemic structure applies to every system, but in some of them it is readily exposed. One widely used pattern—Orchestrator, Researcher, Judge, Writer—maps onto the four cardinals without strain:

- **Receptivity:** Orchestrator. Receives the request, reads its scope, and initiates delegation.
- **Permeability:** Researcher. Opens to external material and absorbs content.
- **Fronterization:** Judge. Applies boundaries that determine what may enter the final content.
- **Proceptivity:** Writer. Takes the filtered material and assertively produces the output.

The axes and hemispheres follow as well: 

- **Identity Axis:** Research and Judgement. These tasks define the identity of the content. A better Researcher makes a more comprehensive content. A better Judge makes a more accurate content.
- **Participation Axis:** Orchestration and Writing. These tasks define the interplay of the content. A better Orchestrator has higher understanding of the request. A better Writer delivers the content to satisfy the request.
- **Orientation Hemisphere:** Orchestration and Judgement. The Orchestrator orientates the delegation process and the Judge orientates the content accuracy.
- **Navigation Hemisphere:** Research and Writing. The Researcher navigates the available data and the Writer navigates the communication with the user.
- **Community Hemisphere:** Orchestration and Research. The Orchestrator understands its agentic community and the Researcher access the communal data available.
- **Individual Hemisphere:** Judgement and Writing. The Judge makes a individual evaluation according to the request and the Writer produces an output according to this individual request.

Operational Status grades the same roles. A Judge that adds light formal refinement stays inside Supra-Fronterization; but if that agent begins producing the outward-facing output, it has crossed into Hyper-Fronterization. The same accentuations apply to the other cardinals. Functional variation keeps complementarity. Dysfunctional overreach breaks it. The compass remains stable while the degree of each role becomes inspectable.

## Relation to Systemic Longings

A shared, scarce computational budget—tokens, context, rate, or any equivalent medium—supplies an artificial physiology that most Agent-Based Models lack. Both longings are expressed through the same resource:

- **Functionality longing.** Agents must perform in ways that secure continued access to the resource and that convert the resource into effective action on the modeling target. Poor strategy, internal malfunction, or inefficient conversion appears as dysfunctionality: the agent remains notionally present yet fails to produce results that justify further allocation.
- **Perdurability longing.** Agents must retain sufficient resource to remain capable of action. Exhaustion is not a scheduled termination; it is the concrete loss of the capacity to participate. Retention beyond what is needed for current action may preserve the agent while reducing the resources available to others or to the shared target.

Because the budget is limited and contended, each agent's attempt to satisfy one longing immediately affects the possibility of satisfying the other, both for itself and for the remaining agents. This is one of the many physiological conditions the Mixed track seeks to represent and manage.
