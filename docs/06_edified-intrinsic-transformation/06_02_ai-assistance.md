# AI Assistance

*On new interveners.*

The clearest place to start Edified Intrinsic Transformation is with the tools already doing intrinsic, concrete work on this material: AI systems capable of reading, organizing, and cross-checking dense, symbol-laden, self-referential text. What follows is an account of what that assistance can realistically do today, kept deliberately modest, because overstating it here would undercut the very discipline EIT exists to bring to the treatise.

Schematic Representation, introduced in Attributes Dynamics as the fourth representation type, is the natural interface for this work. Discursive prose must be parsed for meaning, symbolic notation interpreted against a key, a diagram visually recognized — but a schematic record, built from explicit fields and stated relationships, can simply be read and checked, the way a table is read rather than a paragraph decoded. That is why AI assistance and Schematic Representation belong in the same jurisdiction: the representation type easiest for an AI to hold accurately is also the one best suited to being acted on reliably.

What that assistance looks like in practice:

- **Maintaining consistency.** As terms, symbols, and jurisdictions accumulate, an AI can check that a new symbol doesn't collide with an existing one, that a term's usage matches its established definition, and that cross-references between sections stay accurate — exhaustive, tedious work for a human to sustain by hand, and straightforward for an AI to do without fatigue. This is not hypothetical: the Synthesis symbols originally read `oS`/`iS`/`mS`/`uS`/`cS`, and `cS` collided with an unrelated symbol from Inventive Edification. That collision was caught this way and resolved by moving the whole set to `Σ`.

- **Structuring applied cases.** When a real case is being analyzed — a policy, an organization, a historical episode — an AI can help draft its schematic record once a human analyst has done the interpretive work of deciding which jurisdiction, pair, and attributes apply. A useful discipline for these records, still informal at this stage, is treating certain attribute pairs as mandatory rather than optional: if a case is read through Fronterization and Permeability, for instance, a record with only one side filled in should read as incomplete, not as a finished analysis that happened to focus on one attribute. Enforcing that completeness is exactly the kind of check a schema — and an AI checking against it — can hold a human analyst to.

- **Translating between representations.** A case or concept held schematically can be turned into discursive prose or symbolic notation on request, and back again. This lets the schematic form serve its storage purpose without becoming the only way the material is read: the compact record stays the source of truth, and a full written account is available whenever one is wanted.

- **Traversing accumulated material.** Once enough case records exist, an AI can search across them for patterns a person reading case by case would be unlikely to notice — every recorded instance, say, of an Identity Axis asymmetry that preceded a Unification-type outcome, across otherwise unrelated cases.

None of this reaches the interpretive step itself. Deciding that a given policy is a matter of Permeability and Fronterization at all, and characterizing where it sits on each, is an act of Contemplation — judgment applied to a specific, messy, real situation. An AI can enforce that a reading is complete once it exists; it cannot supply the reading. This is not a gap to be engineered away. It follows directly from the treatise's own account of Contemplation as the approach concerned with the intervener's knowledge-building and judgement, which is not a role Transformation was ever meant to take over.

One further extension is worth naming honestly as a direction and nothing more: a small derivation engine — a program that takes a *completed* schematic reading and mechanically checks or derives what the Attributes Status equations say should follow from it — may eventually be possible. Such an engine would still only ever run on judgment a human had already supplied; it would not perform the categorization itself, for the reason just given. No such engine exists yet, and nothing here should be read as a claim that one is imminent. It is on the record only as a possible future direction for EIT, alongside whatever other applications its readers bring to the parcel.
