## They say

Hobbes's *Leviathan* (1651) gathers almost all authority into an artificial person so that the war of all against all can end. The sovereign is a mortal god: mortal, because it can die; a god, because in civil life it has no judge on earth. Fear of violent death is the ground. Covenants without the sword are words. A reader who has only the frontispiece already has the gathering.

## Conciliatorics' view

Unification Synthesis lasts by gathering authority, even at the cost of function. Representation that does not work is not the emperor's problem so long as the empire remains one. Hobbes is the early modern face of that cut: peace as a product of a center that must not be rivalled.

Conciliation Synthesis is the other pole: function that accepts an ended term. Hobbes can grant mortality of the sovereign's body. He cannot grant a routine, legitimate end of the gathering while peace is supposed to hold.

## Where they meet

Both take civil war seriously as a failure of holding. Both refuse the romance of a people that needs no form. A chamber that cannot close is not a chamber: Operation's fronterization is not Hobbes, but a reader can hear a rhyme. Peace is a load a situation has to bear.

## Where they part

Hobbes wants a last effective sword. Conciliatorics treats that want as perdurability longing in a mixed case. The mortal god is still a god of unification. Madison's angels, Arendt's natality, and the presidential term are not answers Hobbes would accept as sufficient. The handshake is the need for a form that can actually hold. The jobs remain different. *Leviathan* is not a failed Parcels Council.
