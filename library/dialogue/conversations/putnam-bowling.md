## They say

Robert Putnam's *Bowling Alone* (2000) tracked the thinning of American civic association — parties, unions, PTAs, bowling leagues — and named social capital as what was being lost: trust, networks, reciprocity. Television, generational change, sprawl: the mechanisms were argued. A reader who has only the title already has Tocqueville's fear in a later key: associating as a habit that can fail.

## Conciliatorics' view

Operation's south-west is <plain>community</plain>: receptivity with permeability, a choir's shared breath. Putnam's social capital is nearby as a civic fact. It is not the compass. A choir that no longer breathes together is a failure of that hemisphere, not a proof that <plain>community</plain> was only nostalgia.

EIC does not take up social capital as a variable, and it does not rewrite bowling leagues as a Dex id. The contact is the thinning of a practiced many.

## Where they meet

Tocqueville's art of associating, Ostrom's users, Tajfel's groups, and Putnam's thinning can sit with the compass. A reader of civic life expects this plate next to Tocqueville.

## Where they part

Putnam is writing an empirical, disputed sociology of American civic decline. Conciliatorics is not that dataset. Social capital is not Community. Trust is not Permeability. The handshake is the practiced many. The jobs remain different.
