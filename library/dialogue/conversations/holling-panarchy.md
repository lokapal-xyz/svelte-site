## They say

C. S. Holling's resilience paper (1973) and the later panarchy work with Lance Gunderson treated ecosystems as adaptive cycles: growth, conservation, release, reorganization — nested across scales, so that a forest stand can die and the landscape continue. Stability is not the same as resilience. A reader who has Meadows and Schumpeter already expects an ecological cycle of permitted collapse.

## Conciliatorics' view

The Conciliation Pathway is iterated imperdurable functionality: the cell dies, the tissue continues. Resolution's viability is what a context can bear; temperance is potential with a stable support. Holling's release-and-reorganization is nearby as an ecological fact. It is not the Pathway. Forests are not mixed physiology in EIC's sense, even when the diagram rhymes.

EIC does not take up engineering resilience versus ecological resilience as a Dex split. The contact is nested death that serves a larger function.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a health that is only "nothing dies." Schumpeter's destruction, Ostrom's nested enterprises, Meadows' systems, and Holling's cycles can sit with the Pathway. A reader of living systems expects this plate. Ecology as a station is not a decoration; this is why it is here.

## Where they part

Holling is writing ecology and resilience science. Conciliatorics is not that science, and it will not make panarchy an operator. Adaptive cycle is not Conciliation Pathway. Release is not Sacrifice. The handshake is nested, permitted ending. The jobs remain different. Do not import the four-box cycle as Resolution.
