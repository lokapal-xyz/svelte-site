## They say

Zygmunt Bauman's *Liquid Modernity* (2000) named a world of ties that will not solidify — work, love, identity, community as short-term, always ready to melt. The complaint is ethical and sociological: a freedom that cannot commit. A reader who has only "liquid modernity" already has a mood of late modernity as unending thaw.

## Conciliatorics' view

Imperdurability is a desirable pole, paired with functionality, against a biological instinct that treats lasting as the good. Bauman's liquidity is nearby as a diagnosis of forms that will not hold. It is not the desirable pole. A fruit that ripens and falls is imperdurable, and the tree remains a tree. A life that cannot commit is not that fruit. It can be a failure of function, a melting that does not iterate a larger job.

The Conciliation Pathway is iterated function, not thaw as such.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the romance of a solid that must never change. Schumpeter's creative destruction, anicca, Keynes's long run, and Bauman's liquidity can sit together as a family of un-lasting. The family has to be sorted.

## Where they part

Bauman is writing a critical sociology of late modernity's unkept promises. Conciliatorics will not make liquidity the name of health. Liquid is not Imperdurability. The handshake is the un-lasting. The parting is the verdict: thaw can be pathology; iteration can be health. They are not one.
