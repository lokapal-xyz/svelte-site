## They say

Michel Foucault's *Discipline and Punish* (1975) tracked how disciplinary practices — schools, barracks, prisons, clinics — produce subjects who can be known, ranked, and made useful. "Governmentality," in the later lectures, named the arts of conducting conduct, not only the drama of the sovereign. Power, on that picture, is not only what says no. It is also what makes a kind of person.

A reader who has only the panopticon already has the worry: a tool of improvement that does not know how to stop.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Ossification is a reversal in which an edification becomes an end. Operation's identity axis can fronterize (a chamber that cannot close is not a chamber) and must remain permeable (a chamber that cannot open is a tomb). Discipline as Foucault tells it can look like a fronterization that forgot permeability — a making of subjects so thorough that the tool is the life.

EIC does not take up genealogy, and it does not rewrite micropower as an operator. The contact is the reversal and the border, not a theory of the soul.

## Where they meet

Both describe practices that were supposed to improve a life and that end by running it. Weber's cage, Foucault's disciplines, and ossified parcels are not one story. They are warnings that rhyme. A reader who has used Foucault to doubt "reform" as an innocent word is already near the tool that became the end.

## Where they part

Foucault's scene is historical practices of knowledge-power. Conciliatorics' scene is inventive parcels and interstitial attributes. Panopticism is not Fronterization. Governmentality is not Systemic Intervention. Importing those names would dissolve a jurisdiction. The handshake is the made subject and the made cage. The jobs remain different.
