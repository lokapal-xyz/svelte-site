## They say

Pierre Bourdieu's field is a space of positions, stakes, and species of capital — cultural, social, symbolic — in which agents struggle. Habitus is the embodied sense of the game. A field has relative autonomy; it is not a mirror of the economy. A reader who has only "cultural capital" already has the nearby claim: a social space with its own rules, and a tendency to misrecognize them as nature.

## Conciliatorics' view

Attribute Jurisdiction is a bounded environment with a duty: differentiation, not overreach, not a silo, not an amalgam. A field rhymes with a jurisdiction. It is not one. Bourdieu's field is a space of struggle and misrecognition. EIC's jurisdiction is a rule of honesty about where a concept is allowed to live.

A finance ministry writing security protocols is overreach. A philosophical framework telling the sciences what their ceramic may be is overreach. That is not a field theory.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a flat social space in which every practice is the same game. Weber's estates, Marx's ideology, Foucault's disciplines, and Bourdieu's fields can sit with jurisdictions as a family of bounded games. A reader of sociology expects this plate.

## Where they part

Bourdieu is writing a general sociology of practices and domination. Conciliatorics is not that sociology. Habitus is not an operator. Cultural capital is not Medium. Importing field as jurisdiction would dissolve two maps. The handshake is the bounded game. The jobs remain different.
