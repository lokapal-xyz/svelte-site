## They say

Johan Galtung's "Violence, Peace, and Peace Research" (1969) split personal violence (someone hits) from structural violence (harm built into the <plain>arrangement</plain>, with no one hitting). Peace research, on that picture, cannot stop at the ceasefire. A reader who has only "structural violence" already has the refusal to use one word for a punch and a famine of design.

## Conciliatorics' view

Resolution splits friction from <plain>conflict</plain>. Friction is structural — a load that does not sit, a form that will not file. Conflict is behavioral — members at odds. Neither is a moral verdict on its own. Chaos and <plain>order</plain> concatenate with both. Viability is what a context can bear.

The handshake is the split. The parting is the moral. Galtung's structural violence is harm. EIC's friction is a load that may or may not be unjust. A locked joint can be stability. A freeze can be unlivable. The word "structural" does different work.

## Where they meet

Both refuse to collapse a punch and a bind into one vocabulary. Clausewitz's friction, Galtung's two violences, and Resolution's two loads can sit together as a warning about one-word diagnoses. A reader who cares about peace research expects this plate.

## Where they part

Galtung is building a peace research that can name injustice without a perpetrator in the room. Conciliatorics is not peace research, and it will not make friction a synonym for injustice. Structural violence is not Friction. Positive peace is not Viability. The handshake is two kinds. The jobs remain different.
