## They say

Joseph Schumpeter's *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942) gave economics a phrase that escaped the discipline: creative destruction. Capitalism, on that account, is not a quiet machine for allocating given goods. It is an industrial mutation that revolutionizes the economic structure from within, incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one. The entrepreneur is the agent of that mutation. Firms, products, and ways of organizing work do not fail only by accident. Replacement is how the larger process stays alive.

The competitive-elitist picture of democracy is another plate, for another day. The contact here is the economic one, because it is the one that made not-lasting sound like health rather than decay.

## Conciliatorics' view

Status Dichotomies are the first Conciliatorics elements with a clear value <plain>judgement</plain>. Functionality is desirable; dysfunctionality is harmful. That much is intuitive. The desirable connotation of imperdurability is not. Still stranger is presenting perdurability as detrimental — against a biological instinct that treats lasting as the good.

A fruit that ripens and falls is imperdurable, and the tree remains a tree. A fruit that never falls, and rots on the branch, has perdurability of a kind, and it is the wrong kind. The Conciliation Pathway is that cut made practical: iterated imperdurable functionality. Each cell forms, contributes, decays, and is succeeded. Meaning and legacy may remain. They cannot be a demand that this particular form never die.

## Where they meet

Lasting is not the good. Replacement can be the health of a larger function. A reader who has used creative destruction to resist the romance of the firm that must never die will hear the same refusal in the fruit that must fall. Both descriptions treat a certain kind of ending as internal to working, not as an insult to it.

## Where they part

Schumpeter's scene is markets, firms, and innovation. Conciliatorics generalizes the cut to families, churches, protocols, and polities — anywhere a mixed physiology longs to last and to work. The ethical question Mixed Synthesis already asks is not an economic one: would you prefer a form that functions well and does not last, or a form that lasts and works badly? Creative destruction answers for industry. It does not, by itself, answer for a generation that must be allowed to revise a custom and remain a family. That is why the contact is a handshake, not a translation.
