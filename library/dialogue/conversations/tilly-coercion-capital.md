## They say

Charles Tilly's line is famous: war made the state and the state made war. *Coercion, Capital, and European States* (1990) tracked how concentrations of coercion and of capital produced different paths — coercion-intensive, capital-intensive, capitalized coercion. "War making and state making as organized crime" (1985) is the essay a reader is likely to know if they know one. Extraction, protection, legitimation: the state as a protection racket that sometimes became a public good.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Intervention works the medium before the effects. Tilly's states work armies, taxes, and credit — mediums — and get a kind of <plain>system</plain> as an effect. The rhyme is real. The legislator working a statute and the prince working a fiscal machine are not the same intervener. The cut still holds: do not start with the desired state as if it were already a lever.

## Where they meet

Both treat the made apparatus, not the wish, as where influence travels. Weber's monopoly of legitimate force, Scott's seeing, North's rules, and Tilly's extraction can sit together. A reader of state formation expects this plate.

## Where they part

Tilly is writing a historical sociology of European state formation. Conciliatorics is not that history, and it does not take up war as a Dex id. Coercion is not Medium. Capital is not Medium. Importing Tilly as Inventive Intervention would be a second treatise on the state. The handshake is work the apparatus. The jobs remain different.
