## They say

Jürgen Habermas's *Theory of Communicative Action* (1981) splits lifeworld from system. The lifeworld is the background of shared meanings, solidarity, and communicative reason. System is money and administrative power, coordinating through steering media rather than through understanding. Colonization is the thesis most readers will have heard: system overruns the lifeworld, and pathologies follow — juridification, the thinning of the public sphere, a politics that can no longer talk.

## Conciliatorics' view

Interstitial Physiology splits a perdurability longing (organic) from a functionality longing (instrumental). Mixed physiology is both at once. Ossification is a parcel that has begun to run the life. Habermas's colonization is nearby: a steering medium that was a tool starts to set the terms of the lifeworld.

EIC does not take up communicative reason as a test of legitimacy, and it does not map "system" in Habermas's sense onto Behavior Dynamics' System. The words collide. The jobs do not have to.

## Where they meet

Both describe a made apparatus that can crowd out a living background. Both refuse the story that more administration is always more reason. Tönnies' Gesellschaft, Weber's cage, Habermas's colonization, and instrumental longing are not one theory. They are a family of warnings a reader of mixed cases keeps meeting.

## Where they part

Habermas wants a public sphere in which validity claims can still be raised. Conciliatorics wants inventive parcels that serve members, medium, and <plain>system</plain> without becoming ideologies. Communicative action is not an operator. Lifeworld is not Organic Physiology. Importing the colonization thesis as if it were Status Reciprocity would dissolve two jurisdictions that are supposed to stay in contact, not fuse.
