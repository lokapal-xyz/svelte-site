## They say

Thomas Nagel's *The View from Nowhere* (1986) is the book most readers will know if they know him for one claim: objectivity is a real aspiration, and it never fully leaves a view from somewhere. We can step back from our particular perspective — "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) had already made the inner view vivid — but we do not become a gaze with no location. The hard problem is how to hold both: the world as it is anyway, and the world as it is for someone.

On that picture, objectivity and subjectivity are not two teams. They are two ways of understanding that have to be combined without pretending either has cancelled the other.

## Conciliatorics' view

Discernment already names those two cuts, in its own vocabulary. Non-bounded discernment is Objectivity: a river's height from a gauge does not depend on who is looking. Bounded discernment is Subjectivity: the same river as "our border" does. Both are discernment. They are not the same cut.

The method does not stop at two poles. Assertion and Arrangement cut across them, and the working set is six children. A lesion that is there even though it does not fit the model is a property: veracity has authority over congruence. A map that still organizes noisy readings is a formation. "I want this mug" is a preference; "this is what counts as a mug here" is a standard. The family tree is how the method widens what we know without collapsing every reading into one.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the cartoon in which objectivity is the only grown-up stance, and the cartoon in which every claim is only someone's view. The gauge and the border are both real kinds of knowing. A reader who has used Nagel to keep those two in play is already near Discernment's first split.

## Where they part

Nagel's problem is how a single understanding can include the view from nowhere and the view from here. Discernment does not try to be that single understanding. It grows a family, with authority balances, so that a property and a preference are not forced onto one spectrum. "More objective" is not the only promotion available. EIC is not a theory of consciousness, and it does not take up the bat. It stays with how members discern interstitial qualities — and with a tree the two-pole picture cannot draw.
