## They say

Chantal Mouffe's agonistic democracy is a reply to the hope that liberal consensus can dissolve the political. Pluralism, for her, is not a shopping list of opinions. It is a field of conflict that has to be given a form so that adversaries do not become enemies to be eliminated. *The Democratic Paradox* (2000) and *Agonistics* (2013) are the books a reader is most likely to know. The claim is sharp: a politics that has declared the end of antagonism has not ended it. It has only lost the means to stage it.

## Conciliatorics' view

The Parcels Council treats disagreement as native. Article 3: observe friction and disagreements; they are natural. Article 4: revise until sufficient integration; full harmony is not required, so long as no parcel is invalidated. Conciliation is not a new ideology to replace the old one.

That is nearby. It is not Mouffe's agonism. The council is a standing method for inventive parcels, not a theory of how a liberal-democratic people should stage left and right.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the fantasy that a good <plain>arrangement</plain> is one in which conflict has been educated away. Both want a form in which opposition can be held without annihilation. Rawls's overlapping consensus, Mouffe's agonism, and the Parcels Council can sit at one table as three answers to "what do we do with disagreement?" They are not one answer.

## Where they part

Mouffe is writing against a certain liberal consensus, with Schmitt in the background as a warning. Conciliatorics is not a democratic theory, and it does not take up hegemony as Laclau and Mouffe do. Adversary/enemy is not Operation's identity axis. The handshake is disagreement as native. The jobs remain different. Do not import agonism as Article 3.1.
