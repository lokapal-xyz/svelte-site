## They say

Albert Hirschman's *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970) split two responses to decline: leave (exit) or speak up (voice), with loyalty affecting how long one stays to use voice. Firms, parties, states, marriages — the triad travelled. A reader who has only "exit versus voice" already has a map of acting when something is going wrong.

## Conciliatorics' view

Operation — originally the Compass — maps identity (fronterization / permeability) against participation (proceptivity / receptivity). Individual, <plain>community</plain>, orientation, navigation are concatenations, not a menu of "leave or speak." Hirschman's triad is nearby as a portable political economy of response. It is not the compass.

A chamber that cannot close is not a chamber; a chamber that cannot open is a tomb. Exit can be permeability. Voice can be proceptivity. Loyalty can freeze a form. The rhyme is real. The mapping is not one-to-one.

## Where they meet

Both treat members as able to do more than obey or consume. Both refuse a picture of decline with only one rational response. Tajfel's in-group, Putnam's thinning associations, and Hirschman's triad can sit with the compass as four ways of talking about how people stay or go.

## Where they part

Hirschman is writing a small, travelling scheme for organizations in decline. Conciliatorics is not that scheme with extra arrows. Exit is not Permeability. Voice is not Proceptivity. Importing the triad as Operation would be a second compass. The handshake is the map of response. The jobs remain different.
