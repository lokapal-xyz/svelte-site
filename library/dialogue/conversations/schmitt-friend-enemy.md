## They say

Carl Schmitt's *Concept of the Political* (1932) made the friend-enemy distinction the criterion of the political: the intensity of association and dissociation that can, in the extreme, mean killing. Liberalism, on that picture, tries to replace the political with ethics and economics, and so cannot see the exception. A reader who has only "friend and enemy" already has the cut. Mouffe took the warning and refused the extreme.

## Conciliatorics' view

Operation's identity axis is permeability and fronterization. A chamber that cannot close is not a chamber. A chamber that cannot open is a tomb. Fronterization can make an "us." It is not required to make an enemy. The south-west hemisphere is <plain>community</plain> as receptivity with permeability, not as a friend-camp against a foe.

EIC does not take up the exception, and it does not rewrite sovereignty as an operator. The contact is the border, not the decisionist theology.

## Where they meet

Both take grouping as a political fact, not as a mistake to be educated away. Tajfel's minimal groups, Schmitt's friend-enemy, and fronterization can sit together as three ways of saying a border is doing work. Mouffe's agonism belongs at the same table as a later, liberal-democratic use of Schmitt.

## Where they part

Schmitt is defining the political by the extreme of killing. Conciliatorics will not let that extreme be the criterion of Operation. Enemy is not the other side of an attribute pair. Importing the friend-enemy cut as the identity axis would be philosophism with a hard face. The handshake is the border. The jobs remain different — and the parting should stay loud.
