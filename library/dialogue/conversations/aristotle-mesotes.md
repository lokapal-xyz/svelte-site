## They say

Aristotle's doctrine of the mean, in the *Nicomachean Ethics*, is the portable claim: virtue is a mean between two vices, not a mush, and not the same point for every person and situation. Courage sits between rashness and cowardice. The mean is "relative to us," found by perception, not by a single number. The sides are real. The work is to hit the right place between them.

A reader who has only that sentence already has a pair, and a refusal of "just pick a pole."

## Conciliatorics' view

The attribute pair is EIC's fundamental unit: Side A, Side B, and the relation. Symmetry holds them with equal importance. Accentuation focuses on one side without dismissing the other. That is not a mean. A mean is a right location *between*. A pair is a unit that remains two, including when the situation has to prioritize.

EIC does not turn ethics into an operator table. The mean is not imported as a sixth operator. Positioning Philosophy here means keeping the pair's job, and not claiming Aristotle's ethics as a preview of Attributes Dynamics.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a cartoon in which one vice is simply "the opposite of the good." The other side is not nothing. A hinge still has two leaves. A courage that has forgotten fear is not courage.

## Where they part

Aristotle is locating a virtue in a character. EIC is locating a relation in interstitial attributes, for mixed cases that have to function. Hitting the mean can look like a lasting excellence of a person. Accentuation is temporary on purpose. The mean does not have to iterate; the Conciliation Pathway does. Two plates, two jobs. The *Ethics* is not a Dex entry.
