## They say

Thomas Kuhn's *Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962) gave "paradigm" its public life. Normal science is puzzle-solving inside a shared constellation of theory, method, and example. Anomalies accumulate; a crisis; a shift that is not a proof so much as a conversion of the working world. Progress, on that picture, is not a straight line of disproof. It is a sequence of worlds that work until they do not.

A reader who has only "paradigm shift" already has the worry: the working consensus can be both enabling and blind.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Ossification is what happens when an edification stops serving Behavior Dynamics and starts demanding that Behavior Dynamics pass through it. Normal science can look like a healthy edification: a parcel doing its job, with a rule set. It can also look like Scientism: the method as filter for every other parcel, and for dissent inside its own.

Kuhn is not rewritten as a Parcels Council. Crisis and revolution are not Conciliation Pathway. The contact is narrower: a working form that cannot see what it cannot puzzle.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the civics-class story in which science is only open-ended falsification, always ready to die. A community of inquiry has to hold still long enough to work. That holding can become a cage. Weber's iron cage and Kuhn's normal science are not the same diagnosis; a reader can hold both.

## Where they part

Kuhn is a historian and philosopher of science. Conciliatorics is not a theory of scientific change, and it will not call every paradigm an ideology. Some normal science is just a parcel functioning. The test, here, is whether the other parcels still have somewhere to stand, and whether the form can be iterated when it fails. A paradigm that cannot die is the mixed system's perdurability longing in a lab coat.
