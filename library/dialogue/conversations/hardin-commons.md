## They say

Garrett Hardin's "Tragedy of the Commons" (1968) made overuse of a shared resource feel like a law: each herder adds a cow, the pasture dies, and only enclosure or the state can save it. The essay is famous enough that a buffet without it, next to Ostrom, would look like a hole. A reader who has Ostrom already knows the reply: the tragedy is not a law; it is a design failure.

## Conciliatorics' view

Ostrom's plate on this site already took the institutional reply. This plate adds only the interstitial question. A commons can be lived under unification (a sovereign who lasts) or under conciliation (users who monitor, rules that can be revised, nested enterprises that iterate). Hardin's fork — state or market — is already a unification of the options.

Status Reciprocity still asks: is this gathering authority to last, or staying functional by replacing parts.

## Where they meet

Both take a shared resource as a real problem, not a slogan. Hardin, Ostrom, Holling's cycles, and Status Reciprocity can sit together if Hardin is not allowed to be the last word.

## Where they part

Hardin wrote a parable with a policy fork. Conciliatorics will not make that fork the axiom. The tragedy is not Incompatibility. The commons is not Mixed Physiology. The handshake is the shared resource. The jobs remain different. Ostrom remains the needed neighbor, not a duplicate of this plate.
