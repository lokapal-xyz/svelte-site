## They say

John Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* (1971) is the book that made the original position and the veil of ignorance famous. *Political Liberalism* (1993) is the later, and for this contact the closer, claim: a modern polity contains rival comprehensive doctrines — religious, philosophical, moral — that will not convert one another. Stability cannot wait for a shared metaphysics. What can be shared is a political conception of justice, affirmed from within different doctrines as an overlapping consensus. Reasonable pluralism is not a defect. It is the situation a political conception has to be designed for.

The hope is a public life that does not require one doctrine to win.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Conciliation restores function among inventive parcels without installing a new ideology to replace the old one. The Parcels Council is a standing method: six articles, each invocable on its own, none retired once followed. Article 3 asks that friction and disagreements be observed; they are natural. Article 4 asks that an outcome be revised until sufficient integration is reached. Full harmony is not required — as long as no parcel is invalidated, the process can move forward.

The four parcels (sciences, technology, philosophy, the arts, in the field associations) are not comprehensive doctrines. They are inventive jurisdictions. The council is how they stay in contact without one of them becoming the filter for the rest.

## Where they meet

Both treat lasting disagreement as something a working <plain>arrangement</plain> has to live with, not as a failure of conversion. A reader who has used Rawls to say why a liberal society cannot be a church will hear why Conciliatorics will not let one parcel subordinate the others. Harmony as a demand is already a warning sign.

## Where they part

Rawls is designing a political conception of justice for the basic structure of a liberal society. The original position is not an EIC operator, and overlapping consensus is not rewritten as a status dichotomy. The Parcels Council is a method for inventive conciliation — Identify, Explore, Observe, Revise, Maintain, Refine — not a theory of legitimacy. The handshake is about how to go on when full agreement will not arrive. The jobs remain different: his, a political liberalism; this, a perspective inside Systemics that has to stay testable against the other parcels.
