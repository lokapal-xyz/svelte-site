## They say

James C. Scott's *Seeing Like a State* (1998) named high modernism: a faith that a society can be made legible from above — cadastral maps, scientific forestry, planned cities, standardized last names — and then improved. The tragedy is that the seeing requires simplification, and the simplification kills the practical knowledge that made the place work. A reader who has only "legibility" already has the harm: a state that must see in <plain>order</plain> to help, and helps by erasing what it cannot see.

## Conciliatorics' view

Discernment includes a formation: congruence with authority over veracity — a map that organizes noisy readings. That can be a gift. It can also be a freeze. Inventive Ossification is the tool that becomes the end. High modernism is nearby as a state fact: the improving gaze that cannot stop, and cannot hear local craft.

Inventive Intervention still says: work the medium. Scott's metis — practical, local knowledge — is nearby as a reminder that the medium is not the map.

## Where they meet

Both distrust a view from above that treats the map as the territory. Foucault's disciplines, Meadows' wrong leverage point, Weber's cage, and Scott's high modernism rhyme. A reader of government systems expects this plate.

## Where they part

Scott is writing a political anthropology of state simplification, with a preference for the local and the anarchic that is his own. Conciliatorics is not a case against the state, and it does not take up metis as a Dex id. Legibility is not Discernment. High modernism is not Scientism, though they can travel together. The handshake is the improving freeze. The jobs remain different.
