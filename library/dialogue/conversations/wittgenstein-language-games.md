## They say

Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) treated meaning as use, and language as a motley of games rather than a single calculus. A word's job shows in the practice around it. Philosophy's cramp, on that picture, is often a picture that holds us captive — one game treated as the master tongue for all the others. Showing, in some cases, does more than explaining.

A reader who has only "language game" already has a warning about a method that will not stay in its jurisdiction.

## Conciliatorics' view

Representation Types refuses Philosophy's habit of making discursive prose the only native tongue. Discursive, symbolic, graphic, and schematic each cover ground the others cannot. Attribute Jurisdiction names overreach, dissociation, and dissolution when a bounded environment forgets its job. A philosophical framework that tells the sciences what their ceramic is allowed to be is already overreaching.

EIC does not take up language-games as a theory of meaning. It takes the nearby discipline: more than one representation, and no parcel as master tongue.

## Where they meet

Both are impatient with a single medium asked to carry every relation. Both treat a cramp that comes from using one practice as the measure of all the others. A reader who has used Wittgenstein to loosen a picture will hear why the treatise will not let prose do the Dex's job, or the Dex do the panel's.

## Where they part

Wittgenstein is dissolving philosophical problems by attending to use. Conciliatorics is building a bounded vocabulary for interstitial attributes. "Game" is not an operator. Schematic YAML is not a language-game. Importing *Investigations* as if it were Attributes Regulation would be a new master tongue with a modest face. The handshake is the refusal of one tongue. The jobs remain different.
