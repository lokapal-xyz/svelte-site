## They say

John Dewey's theory of inquiry treats knowing as a fallible, public, reconstructive activity — not a spectator's gaze at a finished world. *The Public and Its Problems* (1927) asked how a public can still find itself in a world of indirect consequences. *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938) made the pattern general: a felt difficulty, a working hypothesis, a test that can fail. A reader who has only "pragmatism" already has the refusal of a last doctrine.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Conciliation is not a final state. Edification can ossify again at any moment. The Parcels Council's articles are not retired once followed. Observation names a watch that can harden into <plain>judgement</plain>, and a return to attention. Dewey's fallibilism is nearby: a method that stays a method.

EIC does not take up the public as Dewey's problem, and it does not rewrite inquiry as Discernment's family tree. The contact is the unfinishedness, not a theory of the school or the state.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a philosophy that closes the world in <plain>order</plain> to be rigorous. Both treat revision as loyalty to the work, not as a failure of nerve. Kuhn's normal science, Dewey's inquiry, and conciliation as a continuous task can sit together as three ways of not pretending the last form is the form.

## Where they part

Dewey is writing a logic of inquiry and a democratic public. Conciliatorics is a perspective inside Systemics with a bounded interstitial job. "Warranted assertibility" is not Veracity. The public is not Mixed Physiology. Importing pragmatism as Attributes Regulation would be a new master tongue. The handshake is fallibility. The jobs remain different.
