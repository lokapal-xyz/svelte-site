## They say

Rousseau's *Social Contract* (1762) made the general will famous: not the will of all as a sum of private wants, but the will each has as citizen. To be forced to be free is the hard sentence. The sovereign people cannot alienate the general will; representation is already a problem. A reader who has only "general will" already has the hope of a one that is still each.

## Conciliatorics' view

Unification can wear a popular face. One will for many members is a gathering, even when the gathering is called freedom. Operation's <plain>community</plain> hemisphere is receptivity with permeability — a choir's shared breath — not a will that cannot be opposed. Mixed Synthesis still asks whether the one will is allowed to end, or whether it must last as the people's true self.

Berlin's fear of positive liberty has Rousseau in the genealogy. EIC does not need that genealogy to name unification. It needs the physiological question.

## Where they meet

Both take seriously that a heap of private wants is not yet a public. Both refuse a politics that is only a bargain among appetites. Tocqueville's associations, Rousseau's general will, and Operation's <plain>community</plain> are not one thing. They are attempts to say how many can act as more than many.

## Where they part

Rousseau wants a will that is one and remains one. Conciliatorics treats a will that cannot be iterated as perdurability longing. "Forced to be free" is not accentuation. The social contract is not a Parcels Council. The handshake is the problem of the many and the one. The jobs remain different.
