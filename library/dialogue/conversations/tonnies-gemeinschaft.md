## They say

Ferdinand Tönnies' *Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft* (1887) gave sociology a pair that can be carried without a seminar: <plain>community</plain> and society. Gemeinschaft is the life of kinship, locality, custom, and shared feeling — people bound before they contract. Gesellschaft is the life of calculation, exchange, and chosen association — people who deal with one another because a purpose requires it. The book is often read, especially in its afterlife, as a historical drift: from organic <plain>community</plain> toward modern society, with a loss along the way.

The pair is not a moral cartoon in Tönnies' own pages, but it became one in popular use. Gemeinschaft as warmth; Gesellschaft as cold. The reader who has only the cartoon still has the cut: living-together versus making-together.

## Conciliatorics' view

Interstitial Physiology is the first time EIC lets a drive meet a structure. Organic physiology is the perdurability longing of living things — a body is not trying to be a good instrument. Instrumental physiology is the functionality longing of artifacts — a hammer is not trying to survive as a species. Each type, on its own, can be coherent. Each lacks the other's dichotomy.

Some long equally for both. Humanity is the nearest case. That simultaneous longing is Mixed Physiology. It is not Gesellschaft winning. It is not Gemeinschaft remaining. It is both drives at once, which is why the Incompatibility Axiom bites: functionality and perdurability cannot coexist in a mixed physiology. Seeking both produces either a form that works and does not last, or a form that lasts and works badly.

## Where they meet

Living things and made things pull differently. A reader who has used Tönnies to name why a family and a firm do not fail in the same way is already near the organic / instrumental split. Custom and contract are not the same kind of glue. The pair is real.

## Where they part

Tönnies' pair is often sequenced: then <plain>community</plain>, now society. Mixed physiology is not a sequence. The two longings do not take turns in history. They are simultaneous in the same mixed case — a family that is also an economic unit, a polity that is also a mortal population, a protocol used by people who want it both to work and to outlive them. The axiom is not a nostalgia for Gemeinschaft, and it is not a celebration of Gesellschaft. It is a claim about what happens when both longings are in force at once.
