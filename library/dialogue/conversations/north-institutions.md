## They say

Douglass North's *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance* (1990) gave institutional economics its portable sentence: institutions are the rules of the game in a society, the humanly devised constraints that shape <plain>interaction</plain>. Formal rules and informal ones; constitutions and customs; enforcement that may or may not match what is written. The point is that performance cannot be read off from wants and technology alone. Path dependence matters. Organizations play the game; institutions are the game.

A reader who comes from political science already lives in that distinction. Comparative government, institutionalism, and regime typologies are this site's home literature for a reason.

## Conciliatorics' view

Status Reciprocity pairs an interstitial choice with an intrinsic face. Unification leads to centralization: lasting by gathering authority, even at the cost of function. Conciliation leads to decentralization: staying functional by rotating, splitting, or replacing parts. The questions become usable of any set of rules. Are these rules lasting by gathering, or working by remaining revisable? What does each intrinsic status cost?

Inventive Ossification is what happens when the rules, or the edification that wrote them, stop serving Behavior Dynamics and start demanding that Behavior Dynamics pass through them. A term earns its place next to North only if it still adds a question institutionalism does not already ask as well.

## Where they meet

Both refuse to treat "the players" as the whole story. The constraints they play under are already a structure, and that structure can be well or badly aligned with what people can actually do. A statute, a custom, a party rule: those are medium as much as they are command.

## Where they part

North's institutions are often valued as the durable reduction of uncertainty — rules that last enough to make exchange and government possible. Conciliatorics can grant that some stability is a load a situation has to bear, and still treat perdurability as the detrimental pole in a mixed physiology. "These rules have lasted" is not yet "these rules are working." The field already has a rich vocabulary for path dependence, enforcement, and informal constraint. The interstitial reading is narrower: unifying or conciliating, and whether the game is still for the players.
