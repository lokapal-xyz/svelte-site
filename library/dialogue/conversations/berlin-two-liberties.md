## They say

Isaiah Berlin's "Two Concepts of Liberty" (1958) split negative liberty (not being interfered with) from positive liberty (being one's own master, sometimes as a collective project). The essay's fear is historical: positive liberty, turned into a doctrine of the "real" self, has licensed coercion in the name of freedom. Berlin did not say negative liberty was the only value. He said the two should not be fused, and that trade-offs among values are adult politics.

A reader who has only the split already has a pair that is not a hierarchy.

## Conciliatorics' view

An attribute pair is Side A, Side B, and the relation. Symmetry is equal importance; accentuation is a temporary focus that does not dismiss the other side. Berlin's two liberties are not EIC's pair. They are a famous case of a two that goes wrong when one side is asked to be the whole.

EIC does not take up the history of Romantic self-realization, and it does not rewrite negative liberty as permeability. Positioning Philosophy here means keeping the pair's discipline, not absorbing Berlin as an operator.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a final fusion. Both treat the wish to make two goods into one as a political danger, not a philosophical elegance. Hegel's sublation, Berlin's two liberties, and EIC's remaining pair are not the same argument. A reader can use all three as a warning against the third that "settles" the two.

## Where they part

Berlin is writing a political theory of freedom, with a liberal fear of positive liberty's historical body-count. Conciliatorics is not a theory of freedom, and it has no duty to rank negative over positive. Permeability and fronterization are an identity axis, not two liberties. The handshake is the unfused two. The jobs remain different.
