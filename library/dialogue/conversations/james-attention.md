## They say

William James's *Principles of Psychology* (1890) made attention a central fact: the mind is at every moment a selecting organ. Consciousness is a stream; attention picks a channel. A reader who has only "stream of consciousness" already has the other half: without attention, the stream is not a watch.

## Conciliatorics' view

Observation splits bounded watch (<plain>judgement</plain>) from non-bounded watch (Attention). A chair who has already decided what the vote means is judging. One who hears the next speaker as if the outcome were still open is attending. Both are observation. Presence is a resting point, not a destination; the breathing cycle is the return.

James's attention is nearby as a psychological fact. It is not Observation's whole family. EIC does not take up the stream, the self, or the will as James does.

## Where they meet

Both treat watching as work, not as a camera left on. Dewey's inquiry, Nagel's view from somewhere, and James's selecting organ can sit with Observation. A reader of psychology expects this plate. The station exists because the contact is real.

## Where they part

James is writing a psychology of consciousness. Conciliatorics is not that psychology. Attention in James is not Attention in Observation's technical pair with <plain>judgement</plain> — the word collides; the jobs need not. Stream is not Presence. The handshake is the selecting watch. The jobs remain different.
