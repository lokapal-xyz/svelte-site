## They say

Schumpeter's other famous claim, in the same *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942), is democracy as a method: competing elites, periodic consent, not a people that rules. The voter does not govern; the voter chooses who will. A reader who already has creative destruction on this site is entitled to this second plate. It is the political Schumpeter, not a repeat of the economic one.

## Conciliatorics' view

Mixed Synthesis can hear an ended contest: elites that must face replacement. That is nearby to conciliation as iteration. It is not a reduction of conciliation to a market for votes. Michels's oligarchy is the dark twin: organization produces elites. Schumpeter accepts the elite and civilizes it as competition.

EIC does not take up the empirical voter as Schumpeter's half-informed consumer. The contact is the contest that is allowed to end.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the romance of a people that governs as a single mind. Linz and Dahl, Michels, and Schumpeter's method can sit with Mixed Synthesis. Two plates for one author is a feature: the economic and the political claims are not the same conversation.

## Where they part

Schumpeter is writing a realistic theory of mass democracy, with a low view of the demos. Conciliatorics is not that realism, and it will not make competitive elitism the definition of conciliation. A vote market can still unify — a cartel of elites that rotates without the function changing. The handshake is the ended contest. The jobs remain different.
