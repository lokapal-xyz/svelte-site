## They say

Émile Durkheim's *Division of Labour in Society* (1893) split mechanical solidarity (likeness, a strong collective conscience) from organic solidarity (difference, interdependence through specialized work). The second is not a fall; it is a different glue. Anomie is what happens when the new glue does not yet hold. A reader who has Tönnies already expects Durkheim as the other classic pair, often read as a sequence from simple to modern.

## Conciliatorics' view

Tönnies' plate already said mixed physiology is not a historical drift. Durkheim's two solidarities are nearby as a sociological fact: likeness-glue and difference-glue. Mixed physiology is both longings at once — perdurability and functionality — not a shift from mechanical to organic in Durkheim's sense. The word "organic" collides. His organic solidarity is interdependence through difference. EIC's Organic Physiology is a living system's perdurability longing. The collision has to be named.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a single glue for all groups. Both treat a failed glue as a real pathology (anomie; dysfunction). Tönnies, Durkheim, and mixed physiology can sit together if the words are not fused.

## Where they part

Durkheim is writing a sociology of solidarity and of moral regulation. Conciliatorics is not that sociology. Organic solidarity is not Organic Physiology. Anomie is not Dysfunctionality, even when they coincide. The handshake is two glues. The jobs remain different — and the homonym should stay loud.
