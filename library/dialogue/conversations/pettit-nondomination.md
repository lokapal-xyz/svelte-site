## They say

Philip Pettit's *Republicanism* (1997) made freedom as non-domination the rival of Berlin's negative liberty: not merely non-interference, but not being subject to an arbitrary will, even a will that happens not to interfere today. The slave with a kind master is still dominated. A reader who has Berlin already expects this plate as the republican reply.

## Conciliatorics' view

Operation's identity axis can close (fronterization) and must open (permeability). A chamber that cannot close is not a chamber; a chamber that cannot open is a tomb. Non-domination is nearby as a political fact: a border that protects without making a master. Status Reciprocity's decentralization is a related ordinary name: not gathering an arbitrary center.

EIC does not take up civic virtue as Pettit's republicanism does, and it does not rewrite non-domination as an operator. The contact is the non-arbitrary border.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a freedom that is compatible with a master who happens to be kind. Berlin's two liberties, Mill's experiments, Madison's ambition, and Pettit's non-domination can sit with permeability/fronterization as a family of border-talk. The pairing is the point.

## Where they part

Pettit is writing a political theory of freedom and a program of institutions. Conciliatorics is not republicanism with extra notation. Non-domination is not Permeability. Arbitrary will is not Unification, even when they coincide. The handshake is the non-master. The jobs remain different.
