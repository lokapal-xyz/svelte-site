## They say

Donella Meadows' "Leverage Points" (1999), later folded into *Thinking in Systems* (2008), is the short text most readers will have met if they have met systems thinking at all. She ranked places to intervene: numbers, buffers, delays, feedback loops, rules, self-organization, goals, the paradigm out of which the goals arise. The point of the ranking is not a recipe. It is that some interventions are weak because they push on a figure that the <plain>system</plain> can absorb, and some are strong because they change what the <plain>system</plain> is able to want.

The famous claim is simpler than the full list: do not waste the push on the wrong place.

## Conciliatorics' view

Inventive Intervention is a prior cut, not a ranked list. It is exploration of a <plain>system</plain>'s medium that seeks to influence its behavior. A cook does not influence a meal by instructing the guests; they work the ingredients. A legislator does not influence a polity by wishing it otherwise; they work the text of a statute, the medium through which that influence is supposed to travel. The intended intervention is enhanced by staying with the medium long enough to change it.

Systemic intervention, in this framework, is intentional, expansive, and planned ahead. Inventive intervention is the subtype that works the medium *before* proceeding to the <plain>system</plain>'s effects. Other subtypes are set aside so that this one can be developed.

## Where they meet

Both refuse the fantasy of bossing the whole from the outside. You change what you can actually work. A reader who has used Meadows to stop yanking on a number that will not move will recognize the cook and the statute. The push has to land somewhere real.

## Where they part

Meadows offers a ladder of leverage, from parameters up to paradigm. Conciliatorics offers a jurisdictional cut: medium first, then effects. That is not a claim that paradigms do not matter. It is a claim that, for this perspective, the medium is where inventive work starts — and that Systemics should not absorb Meadows' ladder into EIC's vocabulary. Two plates on systems thought is already plenty. The founding policy still holds: coexist, do not consolidate.
