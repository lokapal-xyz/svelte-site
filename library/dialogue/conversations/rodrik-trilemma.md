## They say

Dani Rodrik's political trilemma of the world economy (*Globalization Paradox*, 2011) says you cannot have deep economic integration, national sovereignty, and democratic politics all at once: pick two. Hyperglobalization eats democracy or the nation; a nation that wants democracy must thin the global market; and so on. A reader who has incompatibility already expects a famous trilemma as a neighbor.

## Conciliatorics' view

The Incompatibility Axiom is a pair, not a triad: functionality and perdurability cannot coexist in a mixed physiology. Rodrik's form is nearby: goods that cannot be maximized together. The goods are different. The shape of the claim — adult politics is a refusal of "all three, forever" — is the handshake.

EIC does not take up the WTO, capital mobility, or the nation as Dex ids. The contact is the cannot-have-together, not a theory of globalization.

## Where they meet

Fukuyama's last form, Hayek's limit of the center, Lijphart's mix, and Rodrik's two-of-three can sit with the axiom as a family of "not all the goods." A reader of international political economy expects this plate.

## Where they part

Rodrik is writing a political economy of globalization, with a preference for a thinner globalism and thicker democracy. Conciliatorics is not that preference. The trilemma is not the axiom. Sovereignty is not Perdurability. Democracy is not Functionality. The handshake is the refused third. The jobs remain different.
