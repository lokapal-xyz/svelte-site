## They say

Samuel Huntington's *Political <plain>Order</plain> in Changing Societies* (1968) opens with a sentence comparative politics still quotes: the most important political distinction among countries concerns not their form of government but their degree of government. Institutionalization — the capacity of political organizations and procedures to last, to be valued, to be adaptable — can lag behind social mobilization. When participation outruns institutions, the result is political decay: praetorianism, coups, a politics of raw force. Strong governments, including some that are not democratic, may be more "ordered" than weak democracies.

The book is a theory of political development, not a hymn to authoritarianism. It is also a ranking that does not stop at "how democratic."

## Conciliatorics' view

Mixed Synthesis already refuses to stop at that measurement. Unification Synthesis lasts by gathering authority, even at the cost of function — the empire that remains one. Conciliation Synthesis stays functional by accepting that a given form will end. Dahl's polyarchy and Linz's regime types are related questions. They are not the same as asking whether a case is unifying or conciliating.

Inventive Ossification is a different decay story: an edification that has begun to demand that life pass through it. Huntington's political decay is institutions too weak for the participation they face. Ossification is a tool that has become an end. Both can be true in one polity. They are not one process.

## Where they meet

Both refuse to let "democracy / not" be the only axis. Degree of government, capacity to hold, a form that cannot bear the life inside it: a reader who has used Huntington to explain why a constitution on paper is not yet a polity will hear why Conciliatorics will not score a regime only as polyarchy. How much holding is happening matters.

## Where they part

Huntington often wants stronger, more valued, more lasting institutions — institutionalization as the good that development must catch up to. Conciliatorics can grant that a mixed case needs a load it can actually bear, and still treat perdurability as the detrimental pole. "This institution has lasted" can be viability. It can also be an empire remaining one. The field already has a vocabulary for institutionalization and decay. The interstitial reading adds a question Huntington does not ask in these terms: is the holding a conciliation that can iterate, or a unification that will not be revised?
