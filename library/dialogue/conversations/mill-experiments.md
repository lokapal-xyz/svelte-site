## They say

John Stuart Mill's *On Liberty* (1859) made individuality an experiment the species can learn from. The harm principle draws a line around interference. Dead dogma is truth that is no longer contested. A reader who has only "your freedom ends where my nose begins" already has the sharper claim: a society that cannot tolerate experiments in living has begun to treat its present form as the last.

## Conciliatorics' view

The Conciliation Pathway is iterated imperdurable functionality. A generation that may not revise a custom is unifying in <plain>order</plain> to last. Mill's experiments are nearby as a liberal fact: lives that can fail in public so that others need not repeat the failure.

EIC does not take up the harm principle as a right, and it does not rewrite individuality as the Individual hemisphere of Operation. The contact is the experimental stance toward a form, not a theory of the self.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a last custom. Both treat revision as a way a larger function stays alive. Dewey's fallibilism, Mill's experiments, and the fruit that must fall can sit together without being one argument. Berlin's two liberties belong at the same table.

## Where they part

Mill is writing a liberal principle of interference, with a faith in progress through discussion. Conciliatorics is not On Liberty with extra notation. Harm is not Friction. Individuality is not Imperdurability. The handshake is the experiment. The jobs remain different.
