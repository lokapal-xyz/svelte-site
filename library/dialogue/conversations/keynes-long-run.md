## They say

John Maynard Keynes's line in the *Tract on Monetary Reform* (1923) is the one a reader will know if they know one: in the long run we are all dead. It was a jab at policy that treats the long run as a reason to ignore present wreckage. The *General Theory* (1936) made uncertainty and effective demand the larger furniture. The portable claim for this plate is the first: lasting is not the good of policy.

## Conciliatorics' view

Imperdurability is the desirable pole that runs against instinct. The Conciliation Pathway is iterated function, not a long run in which the wreckage is someone else's. Keynes's joke is nearby as an economist's fact: a policy that worships the long run can be a form of perdurability longing.

EIC does not take up liquidity preference, and it does not rewrite the multiplier as an operator. The contact is the long run as a false good.

## Where they meet

Hayek's dispersed knowledge, Schumpeter's destruction, Mill's experiments, and Keynes's dead long run can sit with Status Dichotomies as a family of refusals to treat lasting as health. They do not agree with each other. They agree that "later" is not an argument by itself.

## Where they part

Keynes is writing monetary and employment theory, with a policy program. Conciliatorics is not that program. "In the long run" is not Imperdurability. Uncertainty is not Chaos. The handshake is the jab at lasting. The jobs remain different.
