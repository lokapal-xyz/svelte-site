## They say

Federalist No. 51 is the short text many readers will remember from the American founding: if people were angels, no government would be necessary; if angels governed people, no external or internal controls would be necessary. In a government of people over people, ambition must be made to counteract ambition. Auxiliary precautions — separated powers, overlapping checks — are not insults to virtue. They are what you build when you do not get to staff the offices with angels.

The design assumes that officeholders will try to enlarge their power, and that the remedy is to give each department a will of its own and a means of self-defense. Limited tenure belongs to the same family of precautions. A term that ends is one way of refusing a person who would otherwise stay.

## Conciliatorics' view

Mixed Synthesis already uses the presidential term as a concrete structure in the world. A functional structure that accepts imperdurability: the holder, even a popular one whose governance still looks as if it is working, is blocked from remaining. That structure reconciles representation with what a society needs by accepting that the term will end. That choice is Conciliation Synthesis. The other pole is Unification Synthesis: lasting by gathering authority, even at the cost of function — the empire that remains one.

The harder claim sits underneath the example. The Incompatibility Axiom says that in a mixed physiology, functionality and perdurability cannot coexist. Seeking both produces either imperdurable functionality or perdurable dysfunctionality. The Conciliation Pathway is iterated imperdurable functionality: each instance ends so the larger function can continue.

## Where they meet

Both refuse perpetual tenure at the top. Both treat an ended term as a feature of a working design, not as an accident that better men would not need. A reader who already grants Madison the point about ambition will find the presidential-term example unsurprising — and that is useful. The contact should not be a stretch.

## Where they part

Madison is designing against ambition. The problem is the officeholder. Conciliatorics would still have a problem if the officeholders were angels. Mixed systems long to last *and* to work. That simultaneous longing is physiological, not a defect of character. Auxiliary precautions against ambition remain wise. They do not answer the axiom. A saint who will not leave, in a structure that cannot be revised, is still unifying in <plain>order</plain> to last.
