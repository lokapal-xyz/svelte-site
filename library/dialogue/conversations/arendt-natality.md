## They say

Hannah Arendt's *Human Condition* (1958) made natality — the capacity to begin, to insert something new into the world — as fundamental as mortality. Totalitarianism, in *Origins*, was a form that tried to make human beings superfluous, to stop beginning. Action, for Arendt, is public, plural, and unpredictable. It is not fabrication, and it is not labor. A reader who has only "the banality of evil" still has the other half: the horror of a world that cannot begin.

## Conciliatorics' view

The Conciliation Pathway is iterated imperdurable functionality: each instance ends so the larger function can continue. Mixed Synthesis prefers that to a form that lasts by gathering authority. Natality is nearby as a political fact: a generation has to be allowed to start, which means a prior form has to be allowed to end.

Unification that will not be revised is, in this vocabulary, the empire remaining one. Arendt's superfluous human beings are not that empire's only face, but they are a face of a world that has closed the possibility of beginning.

## Where they meet

Both refuse a last arrangement. Both treat beginning again as a condition of a living public, not as a failure of loyalty. Madison's ended term, Arendt's natality, and the Conciliation Pathway can sit at the same table without being one argument.

## Where they part

Arendt is writing a phenomenology of the vita activa, and a diagnosis of totalitarianism. Conciliatorics is not a theory of action in her three-part sense, and it does not take up the social as her problem. Natality is not imported as an operator. The handshake is the refusal of a world that cannot start. The jobs remain different. *The Human Condition* is not a Dex family.
